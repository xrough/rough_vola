by small current-source executable examples. It is not the original sealed
research archive. Raw market observations, full saved Monte Carlo paths,

## Pricing is an approximation hierarchy

European BS, Heston and rough-Heston pricers use explicit forwards and discounts.
American BS/Heston use spot, discount curves and declared cash dividends, with
finite-difference exercise constraints. Maturity-dependent Heston levels are
calendar-time mean-reversion levels, not samples of a forward-variance curve.
A finite-factor rough model, its discrete simulated transitions, its exercise
calendar, its learned policy and its estimator are different numerical objects.

The rough stopping study compared an existing all-state cashflow-LSM variant,
Fourier-premium LSM and one-interval Bellman lookahead on the same independent
valuation paths. The European proxy improved this naive LSM baseline in all six
cases in each of two budget arms. Bellman supplied smaller incremental gains.
The baseline sometimes lost to the known immediate-exercise alternative;
therefore this study does not establish superiority over a strong implementation
of LSM as a method class.

All **36 primary price budgets failed** in the broad comparison. Physical-time
and factor-construction sensitivities remained substantial. A separate focused
60-day study achieved a narrow bracket-and-sampling pass at 196,608 paths and
passed policy-sensitivity checks, but those local results did not certify the
broad panel or optimal-American Greeks. A precise value of a frozen rule is not
necessarily an accurate optimal price. A common dual upper estimator helps
compare policy loss, but a wide bracket combines policy loss and upper slack.

The public [synthetic policy values](aggregate_findings/synthetic_policy_values.csv)
and [paired gains](aggregate_findings/synthetic_policy_gains.csv) preserve compact
outcomes. They are marked archived and are not regenerated by the light notebook.
Their saved trajectories and full statistical audit stay in the local archive.

## Calibration: price recovery differs from identification

Earlier American Heston and mean-reversion-curve studies used clean/noisy data,
multiple starts, spread weighting and independent numerical refinements. They
exposed a spot-grid defect before larger optimizer budgets were useful. Some
exhausted attempts converged after same-objective restarts with negligible price
changes; noisy terminal variance-level bounds also reflected weak information.
Longer maturities improved some parameter information without uniformly improving
short-price or delta recovery.

Notebook 02 is intentionally smaller: it freshly fits synthetic no-dividend
American calls using the guarded exact European-value reduction. BS has one
free parameter, Heston five, and rough Heston only `v0`; the fixed rough values
remain visible. Finer generation, bounded optimizer work, held-out clean-price
errors and separate numerical checks avoid silently treating convergence as
numerical accuracy or parameter uniqueness. The larger historical identification
campaign is not rerun or distributed as a hidden dependency.

## SPY evidence and its assumptions

The [SPY pilot explanation](spy_pilot.md) reports 31 original fits, including
synthetic controls. A conditional no-dividend American-call study became possible
with an independent spot source and an accepted archived dividend-calendar
assumption. It did not validate an independent intraday cross-source tape,
rough conditioning, stock Greeks or hedging. All attempts converged; all 20 market stochastic/profile attempts failed the
prescribed coarse-grid checks. Five later fixed-vector refinements completed
and passed, one partially timed out after nine of ten evaluations, and three
were unstarted when the deadline arrived. No timeout array was accepted.
Numerical stability and errors relative to spreads remained separate problems.
No accepted model ranking follows from these results.

## What is freshly reproducible here?

The three notebooks run from the current local Python source without any private
archive or market-data account. They freshly calculate small pricing/calibration
examples, verify the aggregate files' hashes and perform an optional-input
presence audit. Historical result tables are displays of exported evidence,
not new numerical experiments. Missing SPY inputs never trigger fabricated or
proxy-substituted calibration results.

The unchanged example price tolerance is `.001 + 1e-4 |V|`, delta tolerance
`.002 + .005 |delta|`, with spread-weighted fits also checking 10% effective
half-spread. Modest demonstration grids may fail these checks; failures remain
visible. Current code and tests support specified numerical experiments, not a
general assertion that every model, parameter regime or hedge is validated.

A next research stage should strengthen the naive LSM comparator, investigate
rough time/factor stability, establish historical conditional-state and Greek
targets, and complete market-data provenance before a common-position hedge
replay with financing, exercise, dividends and transaction costs.
